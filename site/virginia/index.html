Virginia mortgage applications · 2004-2025 · fairness audit v2

Twenty-two years of Virginia mortgage decisions, in one dataset.

Every Virginia mortgage application reported under the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act from 2004 through 2025, from three publishers and three formats, harmonized into one schema and checked against the official counts. Denial gaps by race and ethnicity are shown raw, within the same lender and county, and (from 2018) with the underwriting fields HMDA added. They are associations after controlling for what HMDA records, not proof of discrimination or of its absence.

Read this first: what this can and can't show

The limits come before the results because they decide how the results can be read.

  • HMDA records less than a lender sees. There are no credit scores, assets or employment histories in any year. Before 2018 there is no debt-to-income, loan-to-value or property value either. A gap that remains after the controls is consistent with discrimination and also with differences the data don't record. No number here proves that a lender discriminated, or that none did.
  • The eras record different things. The 2018 rule added the underwriting fields and changed how several others are reported. Comparisons across eras use only what every year records (the "common" and "lender" controls); the "full" controls exist only from 2018. The Data tab lists every break.
  • The market changed. 2004-2007 was a subprime boom, 2008-2011 a crash, 2020-2021 a refinance wave. A gap's size depends on who applied and to whom, not only on how they were treated.
  • Who reports changed. Small lenders dropped out when the reporting threshold rose in 2020, and some lenders are exempt from the new fields. The share of applicants who didn't report race also varies by year.
  • Applicant, not household. Each application is grouped by the applicant's own reported ethnicity and race, the same rule every year; joint applications follow the first applicant.
